# Document360

## Snapshot

- Website / docs: https://document360.com (docs: https://docs.document360.com)
- Company / maintainer: Document360 is a product of Kovai.co (Kovai Limited), headquartered in London, UK, with additional offices in Chennai and Coimbatore, India. Founded December 2017.
- Status: Active commercial product, with frequent releases tracked on a public changelog.
- Open source: No — proprietary SaaS.
- Deployment: Cloud-hosted, multi-tenant SaaS built on Microsoft Azure. The Enterprise tier adds "private hosting" on dedicated Microsoft cloud infrastructure with a selectable data-center region for data residency. No self-hosted / traditional on-premises option was found.
- Primary users: Customer support, technical writing, and product teams — mostly SaaS and mid-market/enterprise companies — building internal knowledge bases, customer-facing help centers, SOPs, and API documentation. Cited customers include McDonald's, VMware, Virgin Red, NHS, Samsung, and HP.
- Best company-brain role: AI-augmented knowledge base for structured, versioned product/support documentation — serving as the curated, cited "source of truth" layer for both internal staff and external customers.
- Last reviewed: 2026-08-31

## One-line Summary

Document360 is a cloud-based, AI-augmented knowledge base platform from Kovai.co for authoring, versioning, and publishing structured documentation, with an AI search/chat layer (Eddy AI) that answers questions grounded in and citing that documentation.

## Company-Brain Fit

Document360 covers most of the knowledge lifecycle around a curated documentation corpus rather than an automatically-ingested one. Collect: content is authored directly in its editor, imported (e.g. Markdown), or generated by Eddy AI from uploaded video/audio files; the 2024 Floik acquisition added interactive demo/screen-capture content creation. Organize: a category manager, tags, reusable content blocks/snippets, multi-project workspaces, and localization keep large doc sets structured; an API-documentation module with a "Try it" console sits alongside general articles. Evolve: article review reminders with a "Stale" state, a manual "Mark as deprecated" flag, revision history, and AI-powered duplicate-content detection (Knowledge Pulse) help keep content current and reduce redundancy. Use: Eddy AI assistive search returns a synthesized, cited answer from the knowledge base (on the KB home page, search bar, or embeddable widget), backed by an AI chatbot for end-user self-service with ticket escalation, plus conventional search. Govern: SSO (SAML/OpenID Connect), SCIM auto-provisioning, JWT-scoped reader groups, IP restriction, and SOC 2/ISO/GDPR compliance claims give it enterprise-grade access control. What it does not do natively is pull knowledge automatically from chat, tickets, CRM, or meetings — it is where curated docs live and get surfaced by AI, not a universal collector of scattered company knowledge.

## Strengths

- Mature versioning and category management for maintaining docs across multiple products/releases.
- AI search/chat layer (Eddy AI) returns synthesized, cited answers rather than a plain results list.
- Broad enterprise access-control feature set: SSO, SCIM, JWT reader-group scoping, IP restriction.
- Dedicated API-documentation module with an interactive "Try it" console alongside general articles.
- Actively developed, with a public changelog showing frequent feature releases.

## Limitations

- Proprietary, quote-based pricing above the entry tier with no public price list, making cost comparison difficult.
- No native connectors to automatically ingest knowledge from chat, tickets, CRM, or meetings — content has to be authored or imported.
- No verified MCP server or native agent-framework integration; automation is limited to REST API and webhooks.
- No self-hosted/on-premises deployment; "private hosting" is still vendor-managed Azure infrastructure, not customer-controlled infra.
- Entry-level plans reportedly lack API access and most integrations, pushing many company-brain-relevant capabilities into higher, custom-quoted tiers.

## Best For

- Product, support, and technical-writing teams needing one versioned knowledge base that serves both internal staff and external customers.
- Teams wanting an AI search/chat experience over their own curated docs, with citations, without building custom RAG infrastructure.
- API-heavy SaaS companies that need combined product documentation and an interactive API reference in one platform.

## Not Ideal For

- Organizations wanting a system that automatically collects and unifies knowledge scattered across Slack, tickets, CRM, and meetings.
- Teams that require self-hosted or on-premises deployment for compliance or data-control reasons.
- Teams wanting out-of-the-box MCP/agent-framework hooks today, as opposed to building on the REST API.

## Tradeoffs

Document360 trades breadth of automatic ingestion for depth of curation: it is optimized to be the well-organized, versioned, cited "source of truth" documentation layer — with a strong AI search/chat experience layered on top of that curated content — rather than a tool that automatically pulls in and unifies knowledge from chat, tickets, CRM, and meetings the way some newer "company brain" tools aim to. That makes it a strong authoritative-docs component of a broader company-brain stack, but it typically needs to sit alongside separate tools for capturing tacit or conversational knowledge, and its higher-value AI, integration, and security features are concentrated in the custom-quoted upper pricing tiers.
